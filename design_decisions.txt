Solutions SPHM application

Purpose and scope
-----------------
The Flask application has a public welcome page, doctor registration and
sign-in, draft patient profiles with a risk questionnaire, one-time patient
account linking, and a patient area for measurements, history, and preferences.
The doctor workflow retains patient history, time-stamped notes, measurement
comparison, and a dashboard for manually stored alerts.

Messaging and automated notification delivery/alert generation are deferred.
The existing doctor dashboard can display and acknowledge alert records, but
this change does not create alerts automatically or send external email/SMS.
Patient links and screens are responsive and share the English/French UI.

Application structure
---------------------
- Flask's application factory registers separate route blueprints and adds
  shared CSRF and translation helpers.
- Repositories hold doctor- and patient-scoped database operations; services
  handle risk assessment, measurement persistence, history shaping, and
  one-time patient code linking.
- Jinja2 templates and local CSS provide responsive interfaces. Bootstrap and
  Chart.js are loaded from CDNs; those assets require network access.

Persistence
-----------
- Python's built-in sqlite3 module is used without an ORM.
- The database is configured with DATABASE_PATH or defaults to the Flask
  instance directory.
- Set SECRET_KEY to a stable, private value outside local development; the
  development fallback is generated at app startup.
- Run `flask --app run init-db` to explicitly initialize the database. Schema
  version 2 is tracked with SQLite PRAGMA user_version; version 1 databases
  are migrated to add a per-account theme preference. The same initialization
  and migration also runs lazily on the first database access. Foreign keys
  are enabled on connections.
- Tables cover accounts, patient profiles, invites, risk-assessment answers,
  measurements, vital readings, symptoms, alerts, and doctor notes.
- Passwords use Werkzeug password hashing. Patient access codes are random,
  single-use, expire after seven days, and only their hashes are persisted.
  Redeeming a code creates the patient credentials, links the prepared profile,
  and signs the new account in.

Clinical requirements and limitations
--------------------------------------
- The doctor-entered questionnaire and threshold mapping below are implemented
  from the supplied project documents, but are not clinically validated.
  Questionnaire answers, version, calculated thresholds, and the 24-hour
  initial interval are stored in SQLite. The score field is the unweighted
  count of applicable factors; thresholds are calculated from separate
  moderate/high counts.
- The Flask monitoring service uses the current specification for blood
  pressure grades, the 20-60 mmHg pulse-pressure check, grade-based intervals,
  grade-change verification, and the three-normal-reading reset. It records
  patient-entered symptoms but does not generate doctor alerts or send
  notifications. A total symptom score greater than 5 contributes to grade 1;
  exactly 5 is surfaced for review because the specification conflicts on that
  boundary.
- The old root-level `Patient` and `Mesure` calculations are not used by the
  Flask workflow: their risk thresholds and initial delay differ from the
  current requirements. The patient interface reuses the Flask SQLite tables
  and patient-history service instead.
- Messaging, alert triggers, and external notifications remain unimplemented.
  This is a local school proof of concept, not a production-ready medical
  records system.

Validation
----------
Focused unit tests cover schema initialization and migration, doctor and
patient authentication, CSRF rejection, one-time code redemption, patient
profile ownership, questionnaire thresholds, measurement validation and
confirmation, history, notes, comparison, and language/theme preferences.

Local run
---------
Install the requirements with `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`,
initialize the database with `python -m flask --app run init-db`, then start
the app with `python run.py`.

Patient app design and risk questionnaire draft
-----------------------------------------------
This section records the implemented patient flow based on
`application_specifications.md` and
`hypertension_risk_factors_and_critical_thresholds.md`. The thresholds are
project requirements, not clinically validated advice; a qualified clinician
must review the questions, wording, and rules before they are used for care.

Doctor-entered baseline questionnaire
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The doctor creates the patient profile, asks the questions below, and records
the answers before making the profile ready to link. Ask for the patient's
current information and preserve the original answers with the questionnaire
version. Do not silently treat an unknown or unanswered risk factor as "no";
leave the assessment pending until it is resolved or reviewed.

| Key | Question | Answer and classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| age_years | How old are you, in years? | Whole number; age 60 or older is one moderate factor. |
| height_cm | What is your height in centimetres? | Positive numeric value, used with weight to calculate BMI. |
| weight_kg | What is your weight in kilograms? | Positive numeric value, used with height to calculate BMI. |
| alcohol_standard_drinks_per_week | In a typical week, how many standard drinks do you have? | Non-negative number; more than 2 is one moderate factor. The definition of a standard drink must be approved and explained in the UI. |
| current_smoker | Do you currently smoke? | Yes is one moderate factor; no is not a factor. |
| pregnant | Are you currently pregnant? | Yes is one moderate factor; no/not applicable is not a factor. Use a clinically appropriate, private way to answer. |
| cardiovascular_disease | Have you been diagnosed with cardiovascular disease or established heart disease? | Yes is one high factor. |
| stroke_tia_history | Have you ever had a stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA)? | Yes is one high factor. |
| premature_family_history | Is there a family history of premature heart disease or stroke? | Yes is one high factor. The age and family relationship meant by "premature" need clinical approval. |
| chronic_kidney_disease | Have you been diagnosed with chronic kidney disease (CKD)? | Yes is one high factor. |
| diabetes_type | Have you been diagnosed with diabetes? If yes, what type? | Type 1 or type 2 is one high factor; no is not a factor. |

Calculate BMI as weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared, using
decimal arithmetic for the 25 and 30 category boundaries without rounding.
Classify BMI below 25 as neither factor, BMI from 25 up to but not including 30
as one moderate overweight factor, and BMI 30 or higher as one high obesity
factor. Overweight and obesity are mutually exclusive. The form currently
validates a non-negative whole-number age and finite positive height and
weight; approved upper and lower input ranges still require clinical review.

Risk-factor threshold mapping
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Let M be the number of applicable moderate factors and H the number of
applicable high factors. Apply the combination rules in this order:

1. If H is at least 2, or H is at least 1 and M is at least 2, set the
   threshold to 125/75 mmHg.
2. Otherwise, if H is at least 1, or M is at least 3, set it to
   130/80 mmHg.
3. Otherwise, if M is at least 1, set it to 135/85 mmHg.
4. Otherwise, set it to 140/90 mmHg.

This order preserves the specified 125/75 floor and avoids counting obesity
as both moderate overweight and high obesity. Every profile starts with a
24-hour measurement interval regardless of its threshold. The `risk_score`
column stores the unweighted count of applicable factors for a simple
summary; thresholds are derived from M and H, not that count. The assessment
version and individual answers are retained for review.

Patient app workflow and prompts
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

1. **Link and sign in:** The patient follows the doctor's instructions, opens
   the link-account form, enters the one-time code, email, and password, and
   submits them together. On success, the account is linked to the single
   doctor-prepared patient profile. Invalid, expired, and already-used codes
   receive a generic error. The existing implementation already issues
   cryptographically random codes, stores only a hash, expires them after
   seven days, and permits one successful redemption. Show a newly issued code
   only once, without caching it; do not put it in a session or persist its
   plaintext. The doctor must complete the risk assessment before issuing a
   code.
2. **Patient home:** Show the next measurement due time/countdown based on the
   saved delay, the latest reading and status, and a general reminder to follow
   the plan discussed with the doctor when a reading needs review.
   The disclaimer states that the app does not replace professional or
   emergency care. No automatic doctor alert is generated.
3. **Standard measurement:** Prompt for systolic pressure, diastolic pressure,
   and pulse; timestamp the entry in UTC. Then ask for each tracked symptom:
   headache, dizziness, blurred vision, shortness of breath, nosebleed,
   fatigue, confusion, and loss of balance. For each, allow 0 ("not present")
   or an intensity from 1 to 5.
4. **Immediate verification:** When the hypertension grade changes from the
   previous reading, collect a vital-sign confirmation only; do not ask for
   symptoms again for this verification.
5. **History:** Let the patient review blood-pressure, pulse-pressure,
   symptom, and pulse charts for 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, or 6 months; allow
   symptom series to be toggled and show gaps when a symptom was not recorded.
   Include per-measurement details and minimum/maximum summaries.
6. **Settings:** Patient and doctor accounts can choose English/French and a
   light/dark theme. The theme is stored on the account and version-1 SQLite
   databases migrate to the version-2 schema.
7. **Messages:** Messaging and notification delivery are explicitly deferred;
   no messaging UI, alert trigger, email, or SMS is implemented in this scope.

Patient pages use the Flask application factory, patient routes, Jinja
templates, translation helper, and SQLite repositories/services. Each patient
request is authorized against the authenticated patient's linked profile; a
profile identifier or invite code never grants access to another patient's
records. Database setup and migrations remain separate from route handlers.

Clinical and product decisions required before implementation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
- Approve the questionnaire wording, who may answer it, and how unknown,
  declined, or changing answers affect profile activation and reassessment.
- Define "standard drink" and "premature" family history. Neither definition is
  supplied, so the draft intentionally does not add a cutoff or examples.
- Confirm accepted height, weight, and numeric ranges, units, and rounding
  rules for BMI at the 25 and 30 boundaries.
- Resolve the symptom-score boundary: the specification says symptoms are
  critical when their total exceeds 5, but also says the sum must be below 5.
  The current implementation follows the literal "exceeds 5" rule for grade 1
  and surfaces a total of exactly 5 for review. It does not generate an alert.
- Confirm the clinical wording and behavior for hypertension grades, pulse
  pressure outside 20-60 mmHg, emergency guidance, and doctor notifications.
  Immediate verification after a grade change is implemented; the separate
  consecutive-reading alert policy remains unimplemented.
- Review the displayed thresholds and all patient-facing instructions with a
  qualified clinician. This school proof of concept must not present them as a
  diagnosis or a substitute for care.
